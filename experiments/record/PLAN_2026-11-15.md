# Plan of version 0.2, fixed before any run

Written on 15 November 2026, before the first run of version 0.2. The delivery of
27 November 2026 is version 0.1.1 and is not touched by anything below.

## Rules

1. Every experiment below is specified here before it runs: setting, seeds, metric and what
   counts as which answer. A change after seeing results is written in this file with its date
   and reason, and the earlier result is kept.
2. Changes that alter the existing numbers (block A) enter together and the benchmark runs once.
3. Results are reported whether or not they favour BRANCH.

## B1. The certificate in the acidification category (runs first; needs no openLCA)

**What is known.** In version 0.1.1, with experts drawn from the tails of the priors, 2.6% of the
certified verdicts for Acidification were wrong, with an interval over cases of 0.2% to 5.4%,
against a target of 2.5%. The interval contains the target, so the 30 seeds do not show whether
the certificate misses its target in this category.

**What the certificate promises.** At most tau wrong certified verdicts when the true values
come from the priors. Experts from the tails break that assumption on purpose, so an error above
tau with them is a property of the stress test and not a defect of the code. The question is how
large the error is, not whether a guarantee is violated.

**Setting.** As in the paper: strategy decision flip, three questions per round, N = 20,000,
tau = 2.5%, the 18 cases (four packages, PT, DE, BR), experts from the tails.

**Seeds.** 2000 to 2299, new (the paper used 0 to 29 and 1000 to 1029): 300 sessions per case,
5,400 sessions per category.

**Categories.** Acidification (the question) and Climate change (the reference, same seeds).

**Metric.** Share of certified verdicts that are wrong, with the 95% percentile interval of a
bootstrap over the 18 cases (the interval of the paper) and the exact binomial interval over
sessions for reference. Also the same share case by case.

**Reading, fixed now.**
- Interval entirely above 2.5%: with tail experts the certificate errs above its target in this
  category. Then, and only then, a look case by case at where the wrong verdicts are.
- Interval entirely at or below 2.5%: the 2.6% of version 0.1.1 was sampling noise.
- Interval containing 2.5%: still undecided at 300 seeds; reported as such, no further seeds.

## B3. A second database, ELCD 3.2 (fixed on 15 November 2026, before any run)

**Question.** Do two statements of the paper survive when the unit factors come from another database?
1. Decision flip reaches the certificate with fewer questions than entropy.
2. With experts drawn from the tails of the priors, the share of certified verdicts that are wrong
   stays near the target of 2.5%.

**What the test does not answer.** Whether the verdicts are current. ELCD was discontinued in 2018
and its data sets state validity periods that end between 2009 and 2015.

**Factors.** A hybrid file. The 12 cases need 29 factors: 21 come from ELCD and 8 stay from BAFU:2026,
because ELCD has no separate secondary aluminium, no disposal of aluminium and no plastic conversion.
The table is `data/second_database/elcd32_map.json` and does not change after results are seen.
`experiments/data/second_database/read_elcd.py` computes the ELCD factors from the database file, outside
openLCA, with the method stored in that file (EF 3.0 Method (adapted)); `experiments/data/make_hybrid_factors.py`
writes the hybrid. The incineration data sets used are the ones without an energy credit.

**Cases.** 12: the six pairs of the four packages, in Portugal and Germany. Brazil is left out because
ELCD has no Brazilian data.

**Model and priors.** Identical to those of the BAFU run it is compared with. The test runs together
with the single run of block A, so that both sides use the same model.

**Setting.** As in the paper: tau = 2.5%, three questions per round, N = 20,000, seeds 0 to 29, four
strategies (`decision_flip`, `sobol`, `entropy`, `random`), experts from the priors and from the tails.

**Categories.** Climate change is the question. Acidification is reported as description.

**Metrics.**
- Mean questions until the certificate, by strategy.
- Difference decision flip minus entropy, with the 95% percentile interval of a bootstrap over the 12 cases.
- Share of certified verdicts that are wrong with tail experts, with the same kind of interval.
- Description, case by case: the sign of the central verdict with BAFU and with the hybrid, and the
  number of cases certified from the priors alone.
- The comparison with BAFU uses the same 12 cases, not the 18.

**Reading, fixed now.**
- Statement 1 holds if the interval of the difference lies entirely below zero. If it contains zero:
  "not shown with 12 cases". If it lies entirely above zero, the statement fails with this database.
- Statement 2: interval entirely above 2.5%, the certificate errs above its target with this database;
  interval containing 2.5%, undecided; interval entirely at or below 2.5%, the target holds.
- A verdict that changes between the databases is reported case by case and counts neither as a
  failure nor as a success of BRANCH: it measures the databases. Large differences are known before the
  run (electricity of Portugal 0.78 against 0.19 kg CO2 eq per kWh, lorry 0.067 against 0.22 per t km).
- No new p values, no further seeds after the results are seen.

**Checks of the code, before the test.**
1. Placebo: the hybrid with nothing replaced must reproduce the BAFU run. Done on 15 November 2026 on
   one case (PET against refillable glass, Portugal, three seeds, four strategies, tail experts):
   every column equal except the three timing columns. It runs again on the 12 cases with the test.
2. My calculation against openLCA: three ELCD processes (PET bottle grade, electricity of Portugal,
   incineration of PET) computed in openLCA must agree with `elcd32_factors.json` within 1e-6 relative.

**Added on 15 November 2026, before any run.** An audit of the calculation showed that methane weighs
36.8 in EF 3.0 and 27.9 in IPCC 2021. So the sign of the central verdict of each case is also reported
with the ELCD factors computed by IPCC 2021 GWP 100 (`--climate ipcc2021`). Description only: it does
not enter the reading of the two statements.

**What can invalidate the reading.** The method in the ELCD file is EF 3.0 and the BAFU factors use
EF 3.1. The hybrid mixes the two in the 8 factors that stay from BAFU, and the paper declares it.

**Record of check 2 (16 November 2026).** Made on all 21 processes instead of three, in openLCA 2.6.2

- First attempt: 4 of 21 agreed and 17 did not. The error was in the check, not in the factors: it used

- Consequence for BRANCH itself: `branch/olca_extract.py` as released in 0.1.2 would return wrong

## Changes of 16 November 2026, after a statistical review of this plan

Written on 16 November 2026. A session with the plan as it stood above (commit 16de713, seeds 0 to 29)

Where a rule below differs from a rule above, the rule below holds.

**1. Aluminium: the secondary metal was counted twice (error of the model, found on 16 November).**
The catalogue used "Aluminium, production mix, wrought alloy, at plant" as the virgin metal, beside a
recycled content asked as a choice. I read the exchanges of that dataset in the BAFU:2026 database: it is
a mix of primary aluminium and of secondary aluminium from new and from old scrap, the primary metal a
little over half, and nothing else. (The exact shares are content of the database and are not repeated
here; an earlier commit of this file gave them.) With a recycled content of 55% the model therefore used
about half as much primary metal as it meant. The virgin metal is now "Aluminium, primary, at plant"

- Affected: the nine benchmark cases with the can, the six further cases with the carton, through its
  foil (15 of the 18 cases; only PET against refillable glass is untouched), and steel against aluminium

- Versions 0.1.1 and 0.1.2 carry the error. The paper and the release notes of 0.2 say so, with the

- The same question for the other materials, read in the database the same day. Packaging glass is

**3. Seeds.** Seeds 0 to 29 chose the setting of the paper. The blocks of version 0.2 (B2, B3, A2, A3 and

seeds (0 to 29 and its run with new seeds), so that it stays comparable with 0.1.2, and runs again
with the model of points 1 and 2.

**4. Unit of inference: the pair of products.** The 18 cases are 6 pairs in 3 regions, the 30 cases of
B2 are 10 pairs in 3 regions, and the regions of one pair share products and most choices. Every
interval is now computed twice: resampling cases (as in the paper) and resampling pairs with all their
regions. The readings use the interval over pairs. With 6 or 10 pairs that interval is coarse, and I say
so wherever it is used.

**5. One primary reading.** B2 (other product families), statement 1 (decision flip reaches the

**6. Which expert reads which statement.** Statement 1 is read with experts drawn from the priors, and

**7. B3 cannot confirm anything, and is read by the size of the effect.** Twelve cases in six pairs,
several of them certified from the priors alone. With five informative cases the smallest two sided
p value of the exact paired test is 2 / 2^5 = 0.0625, so "not shown" and "undecided" were close to certain
before any run. The reading of 15 November for B3 is replaced:
- Statement 1: the effect (entropy minus decision flip, mean questions per case, experts from the
  priors) with the ELCD hybrid is compared with the effect with BAFU on the same 12 cases and seeds.
  Compatible: same sign, and the 95% interval over pairs of the paired difference contains zero.
  Not compatible: the sign changes, or the interval excludes zero.
- Compatible means that no difference was detected with six pairs. It is not evidence that the two are
  equal, and the paper says it in these words.
- Statement 2 in B3 is description only.
- What B3 measures: the same products with other unit factors. It is a test of robustness to the
  factors, not of generalisation to other products. Other products are B2.

**8. Order of events in A2.** I had computed the central verdict of the 18 cases under cut-off and under
the formula, to check that every case builds, before I wrote the reading of A2. The reading makes no
claim that one model is right and names the cases whose verdict differs, so what I had seen could not
favour a result; the order is written here because rule 1 asks for it.
- Limit to state with A2: the recycling rates R2 of Annex II are measured at the output of the recycling
  plant, and the rates of BRANCH come from national statistics counted at the input to recycling or at
  collection. The credit for recycling may be overestimated.
- Sensitivity runs of A2, on the 18 cases: `cff_heat_none` (no credit for the heat), `cff_heat_district`
  (the heat replaced is "District heat, at consumer, swiss average, allocation exergy" instead of heat
  from natural gas; only if that factor has been extracted) and `cff_pet_ssp` (quality ratios of PET 1
  and 1, the row "PET - SSP recycling", instead of 0.9).

**9. What the label SOURCE says.** A source gives the centre of a prior. In most priors the spread is my
choice or my rule. `experiments/benchmark/prior_sources.py` counts, per region, the priors of the benchmark in
three groups: assumption; source for the centre only; source for the whole distribution. On 16 November:
Portugal 7, 22 and 2 of 31; Germany 6, 22 and 3; Brazil 9, 20 and 2. No prior has an uncertainty that a
source measured as such. The paper reports the three numbers, not "sourced" against "assumed".

**10. A3, what the approximation does.** Treating the factors as independent has an error whose
direction I do not know: shared upstream processes make factors move together, which widens the
difference between two products when they use different amounts and narrows it when the same factor
sits on both sides. Sensitivity run `uncertainty_correlated`: one draw moves every factor by its own
spread, the bound opposite to independence. A multiplier with median 1 has a mean above 1
(exp(sigma^2 / 2)), so the mean impacts rise slightly; `outputs/uncertainty_report.md`, written by the
extraction, lists per factor the spread, the median and the mean of the runs over the value BRANCH uses,
and the time the simulation took.

**11. Sensitivity runs are not readings.** `wash_old`, `cff_heat_none`, `cff_heat_district`,
`cff_pet_ssp` and `uncertainty_correlated` are reported in a table of their own and with the central
verdict of each case. None of them can turn a reading.

**12. Few pairs: an exact test beside the interval (added on 16 November 2026, before any result).**
A percentile interval from resampling 6 or 10 pairs tends to be too narrow. So statement 1 also gets an
exact two sided sign flip test on the mean difference of each pair (`p_pair`), which is valid with any
number of pairs. With n informative pairs it cannot go below 2 / 2^n: 0.002 with 10 pairs, 0.031 with 6.
Statement 1 holds only if the interval over pairs lies entirely above zero and this p value is at most
0.05. If the two disagree, the reading is "not shown". This is the one new p value of version 0.2; it
replaces the line "no new p values" for statement 1 and is not adjusted, because there is one primary
reading. For the certificate (statement 2) there is no such test: its interval over pairs is reported
with this warning.

replaces the first lines of this file, which said that the delivery was not touched.

## Additions of 16 November 2026, after a review of the whole project

Written on 16 November 2026, after the session of that day had been read and before any run below.
The review found that the paper's strongest comparison is with the Sobol index of the impact (what an
LCA expert would use), where the two tie, and not with entropy, which gives every continuous choice the
same score. Three additions follow. None of them changes a reading already made.

**13. Equivalence with the Sobol index of the impact.** "They tie" is not a test. The statement is now:
the mean number of questions of the two strategies differs by less than half a question.
- Margin: 0.5 question. A person answers whole questions, so half a question is below the smallest
  difference a person can meet in one session, and the paper already treats half a question as a cost
  worth naming. I fix the margin after having seen, on seeds 0 to 29 and 200 to 229, differences of a few
  hundredths of a question; that is why the test runs on seeds it has never seen.
- Runs: seeds 300 to 329, experts from the priors, the chosen setting, on the 30 cases of other product
  families (`eq_families`) and on the 18 benchmark cases (`eq_bench`).
- Reading: equivalent if the 95% interval over pairs of Sobol minus decision flip lies entirely inside
  -0.5 to 0.5. Otherwise "equivalence not shown". The families block is the equivalence reading; the 18
  cases are secondary. This is a reading about equivalence, so it does not replace the primary reading
  of point 5; the paper reports both and says which one matters more for an LCA audience.

**14. A second wrong expert.** The tail expert is one kind of error: each belief wrong at random on
either side. `draw_truth_adverse` is another: every continuous choice takes a value from the half of its
prior (median to extreme) that pushes the difference between the products against the verdict the priors
favour; the side is the sign of the correlation between the choice and the difference in 4,000 prior
draws, and a choice with correlation below 0.02 takes a side at random. Categorical choices come from
their priors.
- Runs: seeds 300 to 329, the chosen setting, four strategies, on the families (`stress_families`) and on
  the 18 cases (`stress_bench`).
- Reading: description. The certificate promises nothing when the beliefs are wrong, so there is no pass
  or fail. I report the share of certified verdicts that are wrong with its interval over pairs, beside
  the tail expert, and say "errs above its nominal level" only if that interval lies entirely above 2.5%.
  I expect a higher share than with the tail expert, because the errors no longer cancel.

**15. Where decision flip and the Sobol index part: an illustration.** In the real cases the two
strategies ask almost the same questions, so the argument that a choice can dominate the variance of the
result without ever changing the verdict has no support from those cases. To show whether the two can
part at all: 100 synthetic models (`synthetic_lca`, 40 choices, model seeds 5000 to 5099, never used), 5
sessions each (seeds 300 to 304), experts from the priors, tau 2.5%, one and three questions per round,
strategies decision flip, Sobol of the impact and entropy.
- Metrics: mean questions of each, Sobol minus decision flip with a bootstrap interval over the 100
  models (they are independent, unlike the real cases), the number of models in which the two differ,
  and the share of wrong certificates.
- Reading: an illustration of the mechanism on models that are not products. Whatever the sign, it is
  reported, and it is not evidence about real comparisons. If decision flip asks fewer questions, the
  paper says "on synthetic models"; if it does not, the paper says that the advantage over the Sobol
  index of the impact is an argument and not a result.

**Limits to add to the paper (no run).** The choices are independent under the prior; masses, trips and
rates are drawn without correlation, and I do not know in which direction that moves the probability of a
wrong verdict. The recycled content of the can uses the dataset of secondary aluminium from old scrap for
all of it, while the 55% of the source includes scrap from manufacturing. Forming losses of about 2% of
the plastic are not modelled, the can has sheet rolling only, and the electricity inside the forming
datasets is the European average in every region (read in the database on 16 November 2026).


## Addition of 16 November 2026, evening: the third ablation of the proposal

Written on 16 November 2026 after version 0.2.1 was published and before the run `uncertainty_consistent`
below. The proposal promised three knowledge ablations. Two are done (no location, uniform priors). The
third, "remove the pedigree based spreads", was reported as impossible because the priors of BRANCH take no
spread from pedigree scores. That is true of the priors, and it is not the whole answer: the database does
carry spreads of that kind, on the exchanges of its datasets, and block A3 already propagated them to one
geometric standard deviation per unit factor.

**16. The spreads of the database, in and out.**
- ASSUMPTION, to be checked in the documentation of the database before the paper says it: the lognormal
  uncertainty of the exchanges of BAFU:2026 follows the ecoinvent 2 convention, a basic uncertainty
  combined with the pedigree scores of the exchange. If the documentation says otherwise, the paper calls
  this run "the uncertainty the database states" and not "pedigree spreads".
- The comparison. `cutoff` is BRANCH as delivered: no factor varies. With the spreads in, every factor that
  has one is multiplied in each draw by a lognormal with median 1 and that geometric standard deviation, so
  the central values do not move and only the spread enters. The agent cannot ask about these multipliers.
- Why two runs. The Monte Carlo runs of 11 of the 30 factors have their median more than 10% away from the
  value the database calculates, which is how the wrong signs of the database show (point 12). I do not
  trust the spread of those 11. So:
  - `uncertainty` gives the spread to all 30 factors. It ran on 16 November 2026 and I have seen it.
  - `uncertainty_consistent` gives it only to the 19 factors whose median lies within 10% (the threshold
    is the one the report of the extraction already used, not one chosen now); the other 11 do not vary.
    It has not run.
